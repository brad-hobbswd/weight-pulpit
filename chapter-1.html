Chapter One

The Church He Loved

Joseph Bennett loved the church, but loving a community did not mean pretending that every relationship within it was easy.

Joseph Bennett loved the church because he believed it should be a place where people could come as they were and find room to grow. The church he served was affirming and welcoming, and Joseph valued the belief that people should not have to become someone else before they were allowed to belong.

As an ordained teacher, Joseph found purpose in helping people understand their faith and ask questions without shame. He cared about the people around him, and over time the church became more than a place where he attended services. It became a community filled with friendships, shared experiences, and people whose lives genuinely mattered to him.

Joseph also understood that a loving church was still made up of imperfect people.

Friendships could become complicated, and conversations could sometimes travel farther than they should. Joseph did not believe that every person who repeated something intended to cause harm, but he was beginning to understand that gossip could still affect trust even when it was disguised as concern or conversation.

At the same time, Joseph could see exhaustion among the leadership. Responsibilities continued to grow, people continued needing support, and the leaders around him were carrying situations in their own lives that did not disappear simply because ministry required their attention.

Joseph understood their exhaustion because he was tired too.

The difference was that Joseph rarely spoke about it.

Whenever someone else needed something, he could usually find a reason to make room. Whenever he needed something himself, he could usually find a reason to wait. He continued teaching, listening, encouraging, and serving while quietly carrying situations that he did not want to add to anyone else’s responsibilities.

Joseph loved the church and did not want to become someone who complained about the people he cared about. Yet he was beginning to recognize that loving a community did not mean pretending that every relationship within it was easy.

For the first time, Joseph began asking himself whether serving people required him to carry everything they brought into his life.

He did not have an answer yet.

But the question had begun to stay with him.
