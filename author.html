The Author

Bradley Hobbs

Writing stories about the things people carry, the places that shape them, and the quiet questions that often remain beneath everything else.

Bradley Hobbs is a writer whose work explores the experiences people often carry quietly, especially the questions that emerge through faith, relationships, belonging, loss, responsibility, and personal change.

His writing is interested in the parts of life that are not always easy to explain. The moments when a person continues showing up for everyone else while quietly wondering whether anyone has noticed what they are carrying themselves. The relationships that bring both belonging and complication. The places we love that can still ask more of us than we expected to give.

Through the Joseph Bennett stories, Bradley explores characters who are learning how to live honestly with their experiences rather than simply moving past them. Joseph's story is not built around having every answer. It is about learning what happens when the expectations a person once trusted begin changing and he is required to discover who he is within what remains.

The Weight of the Pulpit explores Joseph's experience with church, ministry, friendship, leadership, exhaustion, grace, and boundaries. The story asks what happens when someone who has spent much of his life making room for everyone else begins to recognize that he may also need room to be human.

Sometimes the heaviest things a person carries are the things everyone assumes they are strong enough to hold.

Bradley's writing makes room for the complexity that exists within relationships, faith, leadership, and personal growth. His characters are allowed to care deeply while still becoming tired. They are allowed to love people while recognizing that love sometimes requires boundaries. They are allowed to continue believing while asking questions they once thought they were supposed to ignore.

At the center of his work is an interest in the quiet work of becoming. The process of recognizing that a person's past may shape them without permanently defining what their future is allowed to become.
