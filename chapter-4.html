Chapter Four

The Things He Did Not Say

Sometimes the weight we carry becomes heavier simply because we never give ourselves permission to speak it.

Joseph had always believed that honesty mattered. As a teacher, he encouraged people to ask questions and speak openly about their experiences. Yet when the conversation involved his own life, honesty became more complicated.

He did not always know where to begin.

There were situations within the church that concerned him, friendships that sometimes felt uncertain, and personal struggles he continued carrying outside of ministry. Joseph could explain someone else's pain with patience and understanding, but he often struggled to explain his own without wondering whether he was creating another problem for someone already exhausted.

So he remained quiet.

Silence had become familiar to Joseph. It allowed him to avoid difficult conversations and protected him from the possibility of being misunderstood. He could continue serving, teaching, and encouraging others without having to explain everything happening beneath the surface.

But silence could also become lonely.

Joseph began realizing that there was a difference between choosing peace and avoiding a conversation because he was afraid of what might happen. Some things needed time, while other things continued growing heavier simply because they were never given a place to be spoken.

He did not want to become bitter toward the church or the people he loved. He knew that everyone carried something, and he did not believe his frustrations made him more important than anyone else.

Still, Joseph was beginning to understand that his feelings mattered too.

He could respect exhausted leaders without believing that he had to disappear. He could love his friends while admitting that some things had hurt him. He could serve faithfully without pretending that he never needed support.

Being honest did not require him to attack anyone.

The more Joseph considered this, the more he understood that being honest did not require him to attack anyone.

Sometimes honesty simply meant allowing himself to stop hiding.

Perhaps people would understand him.

Perhaps they would not.

But Joseph was beginning to recognize that he could not spend the rest of his life waiting for the perfect moment before allowing his own voice to exist.
