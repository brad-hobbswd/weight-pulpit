Epilogue

What He Chose to Carry

Joseph had spent much of his life carrying what others needed. What changed was his understanding of what was actually his to carry.

Joseph did not leave the church with every question answered. The friendships that had become complicated were still important to him, and the leaders he respected were still human beings carrying responsibilities and difficulties of their own. The church remained a community filled with people who were learning, growing, succeeding, failing, and trying again, and Joseph understood that he was part of that reality as well.

What changed was Joseph's understanding of what he was responsible for carrying.

He could care deeply about his friends without believing that he was responsible for every conversation that took place around him. He could respect leadership while recognizing that their exhaustion did not mean his own needs no longer mattered. He could continue serving as an ordained teacher without pretending that ministry had removed his need for rest, encouragement, support, and understanding.

Joseph had spent much of his life making room for other people. He was beginning to understand that making room for himself did not require him to love anyone less or care less deeply about the people around him.

The church remained a place Joseph cared deeply about because he still believed in what it could be. He believed in a community where people could be accepted, where questions could be asked without shame, and where a person's struggles did not automatically remove their place among others. Yet Joseph was beginning to recognize that he deserved the same grace and understanding that he had spent so much of his life offering to everyone else.

He could no longer believe that silence was always evidence of peace or that carrying everything quietly made him stronger. Some things needed to be released, some conversations required honesty, and some responsibilities belonged to other people rather than to him. Joseph was learning that he could acknowledge what affected him without becoming consumed by it and that he could speak honestly without believing that every difficult conversation would destroy the relationships he valued.

There were also parts of Joseph's life that belonged to Joseph.

He still had his faith, his calling, and the people he loved, but he was beginning to carry those things differently. He no longer wanted his role as a teacher, his friendships, or his responsibilities within the church to become reasons for him to disappear beneath the expectations of everyone around him.

Perhaps that was the lesson beneath everything he had experienced within the church. He could remain compassionate without becoming consumed by the circumstances of others, and he could remain faithful without losing his own voice. He could continue belonging to a community while also allowing himself to exist within it as a complete person with his own needs, concerns, questions, and experiences.

Joseph did not know exactly what the future would require of him, and he understood that the relationships and responsibilities around him would continue to change. What he knew was that he no longer wanted to measure his place in the church by how much of himself he was willing to hide or how much weight he could quietly carry.

He still belonged.

More importantly, Joseph was beginning to believe that he belonged as himself, not only as an ordained teacher, a friend, or someone others expected to remain strong.

And perhaps that was where his next chapter truly began.
