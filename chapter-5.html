Chapter Five

The Place He Still Belonged

Belonging was not about finding a perfect place. It was about learning how to remain present without pretending.

Joseph had spent a great deal of time thinking about what the church meant to him. He loved the people, the worship, the conversations, and the opportunity to teach. He believed deeply in the purpose of the community, even when the people within it disappointed one another.

The church had never been perfect.

Neither was Joseph.

That realization became important to him because he had begun to understand that disappointment did not always require departure. Sometimes loving a community meant learning how to remain honest about its difficulties without allowing those difficulties to erase everything good that was still present.

Joseph knew that gossip could hurt.

He knew that friendships could become complicated and that leadership could become exhausted. He also knew that people sometimes failed one another while still believing they were doing their best.

The question Joseph had to answer was not whether the church was perfect.

It was whether he still belonged there.

As he considered everything he had experienced, Joseph realized that belonging did not require him to remain silent about every concern. He could love the church while maintaining boundaries. He could respect leadership while acknowledging their humanity. He could remain a friend without allowing every conversation to become a responsibility he carried.

Serving others did not require him to lose himself.

Most importantly, Joseph was beginning to understand that serving others did not require him to lose himself.

He was still an ordained teacher. He still cared about the people around him. He still believed that the church could be a place where people were accepted, welcomed, and given room to grow.

But Joseph was learning that he deserved that room too.

He did not need to have every answer. He did not need to carry every problem, repair every relationship, or remain silent whenever something affected him.

Perhaps belonging was not about finding a place where people never disappointed one another.

Perhaps it was about finding the courage to remain present without pretending.

Joseph still loved the church.

The difference was that he was beginning to believe there could be room for him there as a whole person, not only as a teacher, a friend, or someone who was expected to remain strong.

And perhaps that was where his next lesson would begin.
