Introduction

The Place He Thought Would Hold Him

Joseph Bennett loved the church, but loving the place where he served did not mean he was untouched by the weight of serving within it.

Joseph Bennett loved the church.

He believed it should be a place where people could come as they were and find room to grow without first being required to prove that they belonged. The church he served was affirming and welcoming, and Joseph valued the effort to create a community where people could be accepted with dignity and grace.

As an ordained teacher, Joseph cared deeply about ministry. He enjoyed helping people understand, ask questions, and grow in their faith. Teaching had given him purpose, but over time, Joseph began to understand that purpose did not prevent exhaustion.

The people around him were tired.

The leaders were carrying responsibilities that continued beyond services and meetings, while Joseph was carrying situations in his own life that did not disappear simply because someone else needed him. Yet seeing the exhaustion of others often made it difficult for him to acknowledge his own.

There were also friendships within the church, and with friendship came the complications that could exist wherever people became close to one another. Conversations sometimes traveled farther than they should. Personal matters could become subjects of discussion. Gossip did not always come from cruelty, but Joseph was beginning to understand that words could still cause discomfort even when they were spoken without obvious malice.

He loved the people around him, but loving them did not always make the relationships easy.

Joseph had spent much of his life believing that caring meant making room for everyone else. He understood their exhaustion, their struggles, and their mistakes. He could usually find a reason to extend grace.

What he was beginning to question was whether he had ever learned to leave room for himself.

Joseph was not questioning his faith or looking for a reason to leave the church. He was beginning to ask a different question.

Did serving people require him to carry everything they brought into his life?

He did not yet know that the answer would challenge the way he understood ministry, friendship, leadership, and responsibility.

He only knew that he was tired.

And this time, he was beginning to wonder whether he was finally allowed to admit it.
