Chapter Three

The Weight of Leadership

Leadership could be meaningful and exhausting at the same time.

Joseph had spent enough time around church leadership to understand that ministry could be both meaningful and exhausting. The people who served were often carrying responsibilities that few others could see, while also managing families, work, personal struggles, and the expectations that came with being available to others.

Joseph saw the exhaustion.

He noticed when leaders continued serving while carrying difficulties in their own lives. He understood that leadership did not remove a person's need for rest, and being responsible for others did not mean that someone no longer had personal battles of their own.

As an ordained teacher, Joseph understood some of that weight himself.

People often looked to leaders for answers, encouragement, and stability. Yet leaders were still human. They could become tired, overwhelmed, discouraged, or uncertain while continuing to show up for the people who depended on them.

Joseph respected the leaders around him, but he also began recognizing something familiar within himself.

He was tired too.

His own situations had not disappeared because he was serving in the church. The concerns he carried outside the sanctuary sometimes followed him into it, even when he tried to leave them at the door. He could teach, encourage someone else, and participate in ministry while still carrying questions about his own life.

Joseph did not want his struggles to become another burden for people who were already exhausted. So he often kept them to himself.

He told himself that there would be a better time to talk about what he was carrying. Someone else always seemed to need attention first, and Joseph had become skilled at believing that his own concerns could wait.

But waiting did not make the weight disappear.

Joseph was beginning to understand that leadership could become dangerous when people believed they had to appear strong all the time. A person could serve faithfully while quietly becoming exhausted, and someone could encourage an entire congregation while wondering who would notice that they needed encouragement too.

A person could serve faithfully while quietly becoming exhausted.

The church had taught Joseph about service.

Now he was beginning to wonder whether he could also learn how to receive.
